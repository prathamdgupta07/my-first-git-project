How could AI change the future of work?

AI is likely to change tasks and workflows more often than it simply replaces whole jobs.

What does AI mean for jobs?

AI can automate repetitive parts of a job, such as sorting information or creating a first draft. People can then spend more time on decisions, relationships, quality, and creative problem-solving.

Which skills will remain important?

Communication, subject knowledge, ethical judgement, teamwork, and the ability to check AI output will remain valuable. Learning how to ask good questions is also becoming an important skill.

Quick answer: The future of work is likely to be about learning to collaborate with AI responsibly.

How can students prepare?

Practice using digital tools thoughtfully, build strong fundamentals, and learn to explain your own reasoning. The goal is not to compete with every machine task, but to use technology well.

Which work tasks may change first?

Tasks with repeated formats—such as sorting documents, summarising meetings, producing first drafts, or recognising patterns in large datasets—are easier to support with AI. Most jobs include many different tasks, so change is often gradual and depends on the workplace.

What does responsible AI use at work look like?

Employers should explain when AI is used, protect employee and customer data, train people to check outputs, and keep humans responsible for high-impact decisions. Workers need space to raise concerns when an AI recommendation seems wrong or unfair.

Key takeaway: Preparing for AI at work means combining technical confidence with communication, ethics, and lifelong learning.
